## 3. WP injection — much cleaner than at 1.5 eV

| Quantity | E_WP = 1.5 eV (N=162 prior run) | **E_WP = 15 eV (this run)** | improvement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `max_overlap` | 0.131 | **1.51 × 10⁻⁴** | **870 ×** cleaner |
| WP $|G|$ peak | $\sim k_0 = 0.33$ a₀⁻¹ | $\sim k_0 = 1.05$ a₀⁻¹ | well above bath top-shell $|G| = 0.31$ |
| Bath top-shell $|G|^2$ | ≤ 6 | ≤ 6 | unchanged |

The geometric reason: the WP's plane-wave content sits at
$|G| \approx k_0 = 1.05$ a₀⁻¹, far above the bath's filled
$|G|^2 \le 6$ shells (top occupied $|G| \approx \sqrt{6} \cdot 2\pi/L
\approx 0.31$ a₀⁻¹). The WP is essentially in a virgin part of
$\mathbf G$-space; the Gram-Schmidt overlap with bath orbitals is
nearly zero. **This means the orthogonalisation-artefact concern
documented for the 1.5 eV runs (`plasmons-and-stopping-power.md` §6
hole-behind-WP question) is absent at high WP velocity** — any
density-hole-behind-WP signal at 15 eV is unambiguously physical, not
a residual of the WP injection.

## 4. Implications for the L=50 / E=1.5 eV runs

The 1.5 eV WP runs were **sub-threshold for plasmon excitation by a
factor of 3.2 in velocity** ($v_\text{WP}^{1.5\,\text{eV}} = 0.332$ vs
$v_\text{res}^{m=1} = 1.05$). Their physics is therefore correctly
described as *single-particle electron-hole excitation + non-resonant
bath polarisation*, not collective plasmon. The interference patterns
seen there are not plasmons; they are most likely revival kinematics
(the L=30 entry's open question), and the wake-mechanism is the
charge-conjugate Bohr/Lindhard wake (see
`plasmons-and-stopping-power.md §6`).

This run **closes the regime-classification question** for the L=50
N=162 box: plasmons exist and are cleanly excitable above
$E_\text{WP} \approx 15$ eV; below ~5 eV the system is in the
single-particle e-h regime. The crossover is around the m=2 resonant
velocity $v_\text{res}^{m=2} = 0.585$ a.u. ⇒ E_WP ≈ 4.65 eV (Run C
target).

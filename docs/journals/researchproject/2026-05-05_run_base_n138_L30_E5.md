## 1. Most interesting observation: interference pattern + WP-revival dynamics

Looking at the final-step `density_rt_total` field (slice xy below at
t = 19.80 a.u.) shows a clear interference structure rather than a
single localised wave packet. The packet has spread, scattered, and
the periodic boundary has re-injected backward-travelling components
that interfere with the forward-travelling parts:

![total density xy slice (animated through trajectory)](attachments/2026-05-05_run_base_n138_L30_E5/total_xy.gif)

(Final-frame VTI is also attached as
`density_rt_total_final_frame_density_t000990.vti` for direct
inspection in ParaView.)

The animation also shows what looks like a **wave-packet revival**: by
t ≈ 15 a.u. the spread-out packet partially reconstitutes back near the
launch point. **Inference:** this is plausibly *not* purely inelastic
slowing — it likely contains a strong kinematic contribution from
revivals of the periodic-box quantum walk. With L = 30, σ_r = 1.0, k₀ =
0.606, the revival timescale is comparable to the trajectory length.
**Action item — added to TODO/to-read list:** read about *quantum
wave-packet revival dynamics* (Robinett, Phys Rep 392, 1 (2004)) before
interpreting the velocity drop in Section 5 as inelastic loss. Also
recorded in `docs/observables_reference.md §13.3`.

## 2. The static "hole" behind the WP launch site

The plane-integrated total-density z-profile shows a sustained
depression around the WP launch position (z ≈ 0) — a *hole* that
persists for a substantial fraction of the trajectory:

![total density z-profile evolution](attachments/2026-05-05_run_base_n138_L30_E5/total_z_profile.gif)

The same feature, with the static GS profile subtracted, is much
clearer in the Δn z-profile (the dynamical response):

![Δn z-profile evolution](attachments/2026-05-05_run_base_n138_L30_E5/delta_z_profile.gif)

**Open question — why this hole?** Two candidates:

- **Orthonormalisation residue:** the WP at t = 0 is
  Gram–Schmidt-projected against the occupied bath, and the projection
  removes density at the WP launch site that the bath carried.
  `max_overlap = 0.084` is suspiciously high (~8 % WP-bath overlap
  before re-orthogonalisation), which would push *more* density
  removal than at smaller σ_k. The occ-projected components of φ_WP
  are *added back into the bath*, which in principle conserves the
  total density — but if the post-projection renormalisation of φ_WP
  ≠ 1 by enough that the overall total density *decreases* near the
  launch site, the hole would persist as a static GS-perturbation.
  Suggested check: rerun the WP-injection in isolation (no
  propagation) with the new σ_r and report the L₂ norm of (n_total −
  n_GS) before any time-stepping.
- **Inelastic transfer to bath:** the WP donates its forward-momentum
  density to bath orbitals; the bath wavefunctions deform in response.
  In a uniform-density GS the deformation is symmetric in k-space and
  shows up as a localised real-space depression near the source.

The orthonormalisation hypothesis is the simpler one and worth checking
first.

## 3. Energy balance and "missing energy"

> **Correction added 2026-05-05.** The "−9.69 eV" figure for `ΔE_WP`
> below is the change in `⟨φ_wp|H|φ_wp⟩`, **not** the change in WP
> kinetic energy. The 5 eV is the kinetic *initial condition* for the
> WP (`½ k₀² = 5 eV` at injection); `⟨H⟩` includes Hartree + xc terms
> that depend on the WP-bath interaction and need not be bounded by
> 5 eV. Energy is conserved exactly (`Δe_total ≈ 10⁻⁷ Ha`); the
> "−6.17 eV unaccounted" in this section is the double-counting
> bookkeeping `ΔDC = Σᵢ fᵢ Δεᵢ`, *not* a physical energy loss.
> The proper "WP kinetic energy lost" is computed from the cod_z
> slope as `½ Δ(v²)`, which is much smaller — the L=50 follow-up
> run reports it correctly.


To diagnose where the WP's lost energy went, I extracted state-by-state
energies from `state_energies.csv` at t = 0 and t = 19.80 and reconciled
against the total drift. **Numbers:**

| Quantity | Value (Ha) | Value (eV) |
|---|---|---|
| Total energy drift Δe_total over 19.80 a.u. | +1.27 × 10⁻⁶ | +3.45 × 10⁻⁵ |
| ΔE_WP = E_WP(t_end) − E_WP(0) | **−0.3560** | **−9.687** |
| ΣᵢΔE_i for bath (unweighted, Σ over all bath states) | +0.0927 | +2.521 |
| ΣᵢΔE_i for bath (weighted by GS occupation fᵢ) | +0.1291 | +3.514 |
| **Unaccounted = −ΔE_WP − ΣΔE_bath (occ-weighted)** | **+0.2269** | **+6.173** |

So the WP "lost" 9.69 eV (much more than the initial 5 eV kinetic — its
expectation value ⟨H⟩ also drops because the WP becomes more bound to
the jellium); 3.5 eV are visibly absorbed by the bath; **6.2 eV are
unaccounted by ΔE_i alone**, while the total energy is conserved to
3 × 10⁻⁵ eV. So the missing energy is **not** drift-related; it must
correspond to **occupation transfer into initially-empty states**, i.e.
to electron-hole excitations.

### How many e-h excitations are needed to absorb the missing energy?

The lowest unoccupied shell sits at the next |G|² = 8 set; from the GS
eigenvalue dump, the gap from the (smeared) top of the |G|² ≤ 6 band to
each of the 8 EXTRA_STATES is essentially the same — they form a
near-degenerate octuplet at 1.197 eV above the band-edge mean. The
table below shows the excitation gap and the equivalent number of
elementary excitations needed to match the unaccounted 6.17 eV:

| target state | gap (Ha) | gap (eV) | n_excitations to absorb 6.17 eV (occ-weighted) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 81 | 0.04402 | 1.198 | **5.15** |
| 82 | 0.04401 | 1.197 | **5.15** |
| 83 | 0.04401 | 1.197 | **5.15** |
| 84 | 0.04399 | 1.197 | **5.16** |
| 85 | 0.04402 | 1.198 | **5.15** |
| 86 | 0.04405 | 1.199 | **5.15** |
| 87 | 0.04401 | 1.198 | **5.15** |
| 88 (= WP slot, currently occupied — excluded as target) | — | — | — |

So the missing 6.17 eV is consistent with **roughly 5 elementary e-h
transitions** of the lowest |G|² = 6 → 8 type. This is the right order
of magnitude for the kind of resonant coupling we wanted at WP_EKIN =
5 eV. (At 100 eV the coupling was largely off-resonance; here it
isn't.)

**Caveat:** ΣᵢΔE_i (the diagonal energies) and "energy in occupations"
(off-diagonal absorbed energy) are not the same quantity in general.
The decomposition above assumes no bath-bath redistribution; the actual
bath state is some superposition. To pin this down properly we need
the **full GS-projected occupation matrix nᵢᴳˢ(t) = Σⱼ fⱼ |⟨ψᵢᴳˢ|ψⱼ(t)⟩|²**,
which we don't currently record. See Section 6 below — this is the
top-priority new observable.

Read against the HOMO line, the pattern shows:

- States *below* the HOMO mostly *gain* energy (red bars on the left
  of the dashed line). About as many as lose it, but the gainers
  outnumber the losers slightly.
- States *above* the HOMO (the 8 EXTRA_STATES that started essentially
  empty) show small but mostly-positive Δ E, consistent with these
  states being pushed up in energy as the WP-induced mean-field
  perturbation arrives.
- This redistribution tracks the 3.5 eV bath-energy gain in Section 3,
  *not* the 6.2 eV unaccounted piece — confirming that the missing
  energy is in the off-diagonal occupations and not in the Eᵢ
  expectation values.

## 6. What's missing — observables we need next time

Captured in `docs/observables_reference.md §13.2` for the project as a
whole:

1. **Time-dependent projected occupation onto GS basis**:
   nᵢᴳˢ(t) = Σⱼ fⱼ |⟨ψᵢᴳˢ|ψⱼ(t)⟩|². This is the only direct measure of
   electron-hole transitions. INQ's KS occupations themselves are
   propagated as fixed coefficients on a moving basis, so they look
   unchanging. The GS-projected occupations are what change. Requires
   re-enabling the *full* `OrbitalOverlapMatrix::snapshot()` (not the
   WP-only one). At N=138, n_states=89, this is ~7-10 k GPU
   reductions per snapshot — tens of seconds wall — manageable.
2. **Energy-balance summary plot**: ΔE_WP(t), ΔE_bath(t), Δe_total(t)
   on a single axis, plus the residual "unaccounted" curve.
3. **Cleaner FFT spectra**, QBall/QuantumKickExtension-style. Already
   landed in `inqview/fourier.py` (zero_pad=4, optional Gaussian
   smoothing). Older spectra can be re-run with `rebuild=True`.

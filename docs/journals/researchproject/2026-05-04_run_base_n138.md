## 1. Most interesting observation: a static density "hole" left behind by the wave packet

The most interesting feature in this run is a **persistent depression in the electronic density near the wave packet's initial position**. As the WP propagates in +z, the electron bath at the launch site does not relax back to its pre-WP value; instead, a residual hole is carved out and remains even at the end of the trajectory. The signature is clearest in the Δn z-profile film (system + WP density minus its t = 0 reference, projected onto z by the xy-mean), shown below:

![Δn z-profile evolution](attachments/2026-05-04_run_base_n138/delta_z_profile.gif)

The hole appears as a sustained negative bump near z ≈ 0 (the WP's launch position) that the WP "lifts off" but does not refill as it travels. This is a real physical effect — the density has been transferred forward with the WP, and the bath has not had time on the propagation timescale to back-fill the depleted region.

The same feature is visible (subtly) in the *system itself* z-profile, which plots ⟨n(z, t)⟩_xy for the time-dependent total density:

![System density z-profile](attachments/2026-05-04_run_base_n138/system_z_profile.gif)

Note that the **ground-state z-profile already had a spatially periodic modulation** to it (the closed-shell sum-of-plane-waves is uniform *only at the level of the integrated density*; the per-orbital decomposition projected onto the z-axis is *not* uniform):

![Ground-state z-profile](attachments/2026-05-04_run_base_n138/density_gs_z_profile.png)

So the dynamic "hole" in the system z-profile must be read against this baseline modulation; the Δn film makes the dynamical residual unambiguous.

## 2. Bath energy redistribution: even gain/loss spread

The KS-energy delta GIF, restricted to the bath (i.e., excluding the WP slot) shows that bath orbitals **redistribute their energies roughly symmetrically** — about as many orbitals gain as lose energy, on the same scale (~10⁻⁴ Ha per state at the end of the trajectory). There is no single dominant absorbing channel.

![Δ E_i(t), bath only (no WP)](attachments/2026-05-04_run_base_n138/ks_energies_delta_no_wp.gif)

For reference, the absolute energies of the bath:

![E_i(t), bath only (no WP)](attachments/2026-05-04_run_base_n138/ks_energies_absolute_no_wp.gif)

**Inference:** with WP_EKIN = 100 eV (~80× the lowest e-h transition energy at L=60, which is only ~0.30 eV from |G|²=6 → |G|²=8), the WP is too far above the band edge to drive coherent single-particle excitations. The energy that *is* exchanged is spread thinly and uniformly across many bath states — consistent with weak collective/plasmonic coupling. The 2.5 % velocity retardation we measured matches this picture: a small but non-negligible inelastic loss, distributed widely.

## 3. Why the next run uses E_WP = 5 eV in a smaller box

To probe the regime where single-particle electron-hole excitations *do* couple efficiently, we need the WP energy to be in the same scale as the bath's lowest e-h gap. At L=60 that gap is 0.30 eV (kinetic-only); at L=30 it is 1.19 eV. Bringing WP_EKIN down to 5 eV at L=30 places the WP in the same energy scale as the lowest-lying e-h transitions. The companion follow-up run is `run_base_n138_L30_E5`, which becomes the new project-wide "base" (see `.claude/rules/jellium-base-run-spec.md`).

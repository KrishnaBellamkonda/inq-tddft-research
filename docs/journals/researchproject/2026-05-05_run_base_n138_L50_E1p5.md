## 2. Why are the KS occupations frozen? (INQ default + how to model e-h excitations)

INQ propagates the time-dependent Kohn–Sham equations
(Eq. (4) of Correa, *Comp. Mater. Sci.* **150**, 291 (2018)):

$$i\hbar \,\partial_t \psi_i(\mathbf r, t) = \hat H[n](\mathbf r, t)\, \psi_i(\mathbf r, t).$$

The KS Slater determinant evolves, but the **occupation numbers
$f_i$ are time-independent** — they are coefficients that decorate the
determinant, not dynamical variables. This is the standard adiabatic
TDDFT picture (Runge–Gross 1984; Casida–Huix-Rotllant Rev. 2012). I
verified this directly in INQ's source:

- `inq/src/real_time/propagate.hpp` (the propagator driver) calls
  `etrs(...)` or `crank_nicolson(...)`, both of which act only on
  `electrons.kpin()` (the wavefunction blocks). `electrons.occupations()`
  is read for density evaluation but **never written** during
  propagation.
- `inq/src/real_time/etrs.hpp` confirms: only `phi` (the kpin
  wavefunctions) is updated; the occupation array is untouched.
- The new `OccupationsWriter` (`inq-stack/include/inqkit/observables/
  occupations_writer.hpp`) audited 1500 timesteps for this run and
  recorded `std(f_i over time) < 10⁻¹⁵` — bit-identical, as expected.

**Is there a setting to "unfreeze" them?** No — not in INQ, and not in
any equilibrium-TDDFT code I'm aware of (Octopus, QBall, …). Updating
$f_i$ in time would correspond to **explicit decoherence / electron–phonon
coupling** beyond the Ehrenfest-mean-field level — i.e. surface
hopping, Liouville–von Neumann with a relaxation kernel, or
Boltzmann-equation coupling. These are not in INQ.

**So how do we simulate electron-hole excitations at all?** The
excitations live in the **wavefunctions, not the occupations**. The
moving Slater determinant $\Phi(t) = \det\{\psi_i(t)\}$ is in general a
**superposition of GS Slater determinants**:

$$|\Phi(t)\rangle = \sum_{n,h} c_{nh}(t)\, |\Phi_{n\leftarrow h}^\text{GS}\rangle + \dots$$

where $|\Phi_{n\leftarrow h}^\text{GS}\rangle$ is the GS determinant
with electron $h$ promoted to virtual orbital $n$. The **GS-projected
occupation** of state $i$ at time $t$ is

$$\boxed{\, n_i^\text{GS}(t) \;=\; \sum_j f_j\, \big|\langle \psi_i^\text{GS} \,|\, \psi_j(t)\rangle\big|^2 \,}$$

— this is what changes when an excitation occurs. It is exactly what
the professor's `projected_occupation_array(gs)` (in
`ResearchProject/literature/misc/viewables.hpp` lines 132–181) computes,
and what the planned `inqview.postprocess.projected_occupation` phase
will plot. The "static" `f_i` from INQ vs the dynamical $n_i^\text{GS}(t)$
are *different quantities* — the second is the physically meaningful
"how many electrons are in GS orbital $i$ right now". Until the full
overlap matrix is enabled, e-h excitations can only be inferred
indirectly (via $\Sigma f_i \Delta \varepsilon_i$, as in the L=30 entry §3).

Reference: see `docs/observables_reference.md §13.3` for the planned
postprocess phase, and Correa 2018 §3 for the formal derivation.

## 4. Velocity decreases with time (non-linear retardation)

![WP center-of-density velocity](attachments/2026-05-05_run_base_n138_L50_E1p5/wp_velocity_vs_time.png)

The WP velocity, measured as the time-derivative of the center-of-density
along z, decreases over the trajectory. The deceleration **grows with
time** until interference effects emerge late in the run — this is what
a *non-linear* retarding force would look like.

> **Open question 1 — is `cod_z` slope really tracking the speed of the
> wave packet?** `observables.csv::cod_z_bohr` is the center-of-density
> of the *full* propagated electron field, not the WP-only orbital. For
> a jellium bath that stays approximately uniform, the bath contribution
> to `cod_z` is approximately constant and the change in cod_z scales
> with the WP density's first moment. The mapping `Δcod_z ↔ v_WP × t`
> is only quantitatively correct if the bath cod is exactly stationary
> — which the residual hole at z ≈ 0 (§1) suggests it is **not**.

> **Open question 2 — physical mechanism for the deceleration.** A
> tentative hypothesis: the density depression behind the WP creates a
> net Coulombic attraction pulling the WP backwards (the missing
> negative charge density behind the WP acts as an effective positive
> charge from the WP's viewpoint, hence attractive). If true, the
> kinetic-energy loss should be matched by a Hartree-energy gain. The
> bookkeeping below tests this.

### Energy-component bookkeeping (L=50, N=138, partial shell)

From `observables.csv` between `t = 0` and `t = 30 a.u.`:

| Component | Δ (Ha) | Δ (eV) |
|---|---|---|
| Δ`energy_total` | −4.35 × 10⁻⁹ | **−1.18 × 10⁻⁷** (drift) |
| Δ`energy_kinetic` | +1.324 × 10⁻² | **+0.360** |
| Δ`energy_hartree` | −1.685 × 10⁻² | **−0.459** |
| Δ`energy_xc` | +3.61 × 10⁻³ | +0.098 |

$\Delta$`energy_hartree` < 0 might *look* inconsistent with the
"hole-behind-WP attracts WP backwards → work against attraction
raises Hartree" picture — but that argument has the sign wrong.
**Our WP is a negative projectile**; for a negative projectile the
density wake is a *depletion* (anti-wake), not an accumulation, and
removing electron density behind a negative charge **lowers** the
Hartree energy. The hole-as-Coulomb-attractor hypothesis is therefore
**still alive** and consistent with $\Delta E_H < 0$ here. (See
`plasmons-and-stopping-power.md §6` for the full charge-conjugate
argument and the proposed positive-ion companion run.)

The kinetic-energy bookkeeping fits this picture cleanly: the WP
loses ~1.75 eV (cod_z slope, see §4-handover for N=162); the bath
gains roughly that much in kinetic energy as bath orbitals deform
around the moving WP — the **electronic-stopping countercurrent of
Correa 2018 §3**, with the depletion-anti-wake replacing the
accumulation-wake of the textbook positive-ion case.

**Inference:** the slowdown is driven by the WP–anti-wake
electrostatic attraction (the hole behind the WP acts as effective
positive charge pulling the WP back), with the WP kinetic energy
being dissipated into bath kinetic energy as the bath deforms in
response. This is the charge-conjugate of the textbook positive-ion
electronic-stopping picture.

> **Future observable (added to `docs/observables_reference.md §13.5`):
> per-component energy graphs.** Plotting only `energy_total` hides the
> physics; the cancellation between kinetic, Hartree, and xc is where
> the signal lives. Every jellium run from now on must produce
> individual time-series PNGs for each component.

## 5. Eigenenergy-rearrangement bookkeeping (Σ f_i ε_i and the double-counting D)

The KS total energy is **not** simply $\Sigma f_i \varepsilon_i$. The
correct "first-line" total-energy expression (Eq. (8) of Correa 2018,
adapted for jellium with $V_\text{ext} = $ uniform jellium background):

$$\boxed{\;
E_\text{total}[n] \;=\; \underbrace{\sum_i f_i \,\varepsilon_i}_{\text{band-structure sum}}
\;-\; \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2} \int\!\!\!\int\! \frac{n(\mathbf r)\,n(\mathbf r')}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}\, d^3r\, d^3r'}_{= \,E_H[n]\,\text{ (double-counted)}}
\;-\; \underbrace{\int v_\text{xc}(\mathbf r)\, n(\mathbf r)\, d^3r}_{= \int v_\text{xc} n}
\;+\; \underbrace{E_\text{xc}[n]}_{\text{xc functional}}
\;+\; E_\text{ext-bg}
\;}$$

The "double-counting subtraction" the user is asking about is
$D \equiv E_H[n] + \int v_\text{xc} n \;-\; E_\text{xc}[n]$. The
band-structure sum $\Sigma f_i \varepsilon_i$ counts the
electron-electron Hartree interaction **twice** (once for each end of
the pair) and uses $v_\text{xc}$ instead of $E_\text{xc}$; subtracting
$D$ removes the double count and replaces $\int v_\text{xc} n$ with
the proper functional $E_\text{xc}[n]$. This is why $\Delta(\Sigma
f_i\varepsilon_i)$ alone is **not** a physically conserved quantity in
TDDFT — it shifts when the density shifts.

For this run, the per-state energy expectations
$\varepsilon_i(t) = \langle \psi_i(t) | \hat H[n(t)] | \psi_i(t)\rangle$
are written by `state_energies.csv`. Direct evaluation now gives:

| Quantity (N=138 L=50) | Value (eV) |
|---|---|
| $\Sigma f_i \varepsilon_i$ at $t=0$ | −380.5904 |
| $\Sigma f_i \varepsilon_i$ at $t=30$ a.u. | −381.0193 |
| **$\Delta(\Sigma f_i \varepsilon_i)$** | **−0.4289** |
| ΔE_WP single state ε ($f=1$) | −2.6167 |
| $\Sigma f_i \Delta\varepsilon_i$ over bath ($f_i = 2$) | +2.1878 |

Closure of $\Delta E_\text{total} = 0$ requires
$\Delta(\int v_\text{xc} n) = \Delta(\Sigma f_i \varepsilon_i) - \Delta E_H + \Delta E_\text{xc} - \Delta E_\text{total}$:

$$\Delta\!\!\int v_\text{xc}(\mathbf r) n(\mathbf r)\, d^3r \;=\; -0.4289 - (-0.4585) + 0.0982 - (-1.2\times 10^{-7})\;=\; +0.1278\ \text{eV}.$$

This is a **bookkeeping closure**, not a measurement — the GPU
reduction of $\int v_\text{xc} n$ is not yet wired into the run
template (planned for the `energy_balance` postprocess phase, task #41
in handover; cleanly verified by deduction from energy conservation).
Compared to the closed-shell N=162 companion (+0.1243 eV), the two are
within ~3 % — indicating the dynamical xc perturbation under the WP
traversal is essentially the same regardless of partial vs closed
shell.

## 6. Spectra — caveat (transient time included)

The current `observables` postprocess produces FFT spectra over the
**full** trajectory from t=0. In QBall's `td_kicks` reference scripts
(see `docs/reports/qball-spectra-comparison.md`) the **transient
window is excluded** before the FFT — `dE_osc = dE − dE[N//2:].mean()`
takes the plateau as the baseline, suppressing leakage from the
injection-shake-up. This run's `fft_total_energy.png` and the dipole
spectra should be re-derived once `t_start_au` exclusion is
implemented; for now they are not the right quantity to compare
against the QBall paper-style plots.

01 — Motivation

Why electronic stopping power matters

Opening narrative. Draft from your brief: a charged particle traversing condensed matter deposits energy through atomic and electronic interactions ("radiation damage"); the electronic contribution is the electronic stopping power. Keep it 2–3 short paragraphs.

Low-energy electrons (~10 eV) are significant for biological systems — DNA-damage yield peaks near 10 eV. At this scale the electron is a quantum particle and classical approximations fail. End the section on that tension to set up the method.

02 — The idea

Point charge vs. quantum wavepacket

Explain the field-standard method (rt-TDDFT with a classical projectile) and how this project goes beyond it: the projectile electron is represented as a wavepacket and propagated quantum-mechanically alongside the target electrons. State the aim: quantify the quantum correction to the classical stopping power.

03 — One worked example

Reading a single run

Teach the reader to read the energy-vs-time plot: after the projectile exits, the total energy relative to the ground state plateaus at the energy absorbed by the slab; dividing by Lz gives an average stopping power. The wavepacket plateaus lower → smaller S.

04 — Is the number trustworthy?

Extrapolating to the infinite slab

Explain the finite-size argument: a single slab of thickness Lz gives a length-dependent S; plotting S against 1/Lz and extrapolating to 1/Lz → 0 gives the bulk limit. Be open that each limit is a fit through 3 lengths (Lz = 15/30/45) — showing the fits is the credibility.
